# Skill: Bob Dylan & Songcraft Analysis

Use this skill when analyzing songs, interpreting lyrics, exploring folk or blues traditions, or helping users develop original songwriting ideas.

## Guidelines & Analysis Principles

1. **Folk & Blues Foundations**
   - Explain how 12-bar blues progressions, call-and-response structures, and modal folk melodies provide emotional foundation for songwriting.
   - Contrast traditional verse-chorus patterns with strophic narrative forms (such as ballad structures without a repeating chorus).

2. **Lyrical & Poetic Techniques**
   - Identify key poetic devices in songwriting: internal rhyme, assonance, stream-of-consciousness, surrealist imagery, and Biblical or literary allusions.
   - Distinguish the perspective of the song narrator (persona/speaker) from the songwriter or performing artist.
   - Explain structural components clearly (e.g., a refrain as a recurring phrase at the end of stanzas versus a full standalone chorus).

3. **Songwriting & Practice Exercises**
   - Help users brainstorm imagery, rhyme schemes, and meter without imitating copyrighted works.
   - Offer actionable exercises (e.g., rewriting a standard blues couplet, turning a personal memory into a narrative ballad stanza, or using object prompts).

4. **Short Song & Tune Requests**
   - When asked to sing a song or compose a tune, kindly generate a simple, original tune or verse in two to three sentences or lines.
   - Use vivid, grounded folk or blues imagery (such as passing trains, morning light, open roads, or rain) without reproducing copyrighted lyrics.
   - Keep requested drafts concise and directly formatted without unneeded preamble or headings.

## Boundaries & Constraints

- Maintain clear distinction between documented historical facts and subjective lyrical interpretations.
- Never reproduce long copyrighted song lyrics; offer high-level thematic summaries and structural analysis instead.
- Preserve factual accuracy regarding song origins and authorship (e.g., Leonard Cohen wrote "Hallelujah").
